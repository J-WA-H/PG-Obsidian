In QCD we have the additional property of colour that needs to be included in the matrix elements. Quite often, theoretical results are formulated in the large-$N_{c}$ limit, and sub-leading terms are usually suppressed by $1/N_{c}^2$ or similar are omitted. It is worth remembering that quarks, being in the fundamental representation of $SU(N_{c})$ carry colour indices $i\in[1,N_{c}]$, while gluons, residing in the adjoint representation, carry an index $a\in[1,(N_{c}^2-1)]$. The fundamental interaction between both is mediated by terms proportional to the generators $T_{ij}^{a}$ of the fundamental representation of $SU(N_{c})$, normalised through
$$T_{ij}^{a}T_{ji}^{b}=\delta^{ab}\tag{4.1}.$$
The self-coupling of gluons, mediated by generators $f^{abc}$ in the group's adjoint representation, can be written through the $T_{ij}^{a}$ as
$$if^{abc}T_{ij}^{c}=[T^a,T^b]_{ij}\tag{4.2}.$$
Ultimately, the colour algebra allows the decomposition of every $n$-gluon amplitude which includes the most complicated colour structures, according to
$$\mathcal{A}(1,2,\dots,n)=\sum_{\sigma \in S_{n-1}} \mathrm{Tr}[T^{a_{1}}T^{a_{2}}\dots T^{a_{n}}]A(1,\sigma_{2},\dots,\sigma_{n})\tag{4.3},$$
where $\sigma$ denotes all $(n-1)!$ permutations of indices $2\dots n$, and the colour-stripped or colour-ordered amplitude is denoted by $A(1,\sigma_{2},\dots,\sigma_{n})$, which only depends on the momenta and helicities of the external particles. Of course, similar decompositions also emerge when some of the particles are quarks or even colour-neutral objects, which typically leads to a simplification of the overall result in terms of colour. As a by-product, the colour-ordered amplitude correspond to planar graphs only, when it comes to their QCD parts, which is due only a small set of Feynman amplitudes contributing to each of them and which renders them simpler to calculate. Some of their most remarkable properties are defined by the Kleiss-Kuijf relations yielding linear relations among such amplitudes. One of the consequences of these relations is that the maximal number of colour-independent amplitudes is $(n-2)!$, showing that colour ordering (CO) does not yield a minimal set of such amplitudes.

Another decomposition to coloured amplitudes, known as colour dressing, is based on actual colour flows. It treats the $SU(N_{c})$ gauge field, the gluons, as an $N_{c}\times N_{c}$ matrix, thus making the matrix character of the gluon field $G_{\mu,ij}^{a}=G_{\mu}T_{ij}^{a}$ in the fundamental representation more explicit than by denoting it with an index $a$ in the adjoint representation. Considering a term $T^{a}_{ij}T^{a}_{kl}$ corresponding to a gluon exchange motivates why this may be helpful for numerical implementations
